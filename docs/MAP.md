# Login page landscape — how it works

The sign-in page (`index.html`) sits on top of one continuous isometric map drawn as a single inline SVG.
Everything is generated by JavaScript at load time; there are no image files in the map except the
artwork you import from `exports/` (see the last section).

## Files

| File | What it does |
|---|---|
| `index.html` | Page shell: glass header, sign-in card, glass footer with the logos, CSS animation classes. Loads the scripts below in order, then calls `Assets.map.render()` into `<svg id="map">`. |
| `assets/core.js` | `window.Assets` registry, shared `<defs>` (`Assets.addDefs`, `Assets.mountDefs`). |
| `assets/iso.js` | Isometric helpers: `P(x,y,z)` projection, `box`, `cyl`, `poly`, `T`, `label`, `lamp`, `person`, `fence`, `packet`, `C` colour sets. |
| `assets/silos.js`, `marine.js`, `tanks.js`, `trucks.js`, `cars.js`, `houses.js`, `buildings.js`, `terrain.js` | The individual drawn assets (cooling towers, ships, tanks, vehicles, houses, school, rondavels, city buildings, ground patterns). |
| `assets/textures.js` | Paints the ground textures once at load on a `<canvas>` (tileable noise + grass blades, cracks, pebbles…) and registers them as `tx-*` patterns. See *Land textures* below. |
| `assets/exports-manifest.js` | **Generated.** List of every file in `exports/svg` with its original frame. |
| `assets/exports.js` | `Assets.ex` — places an exported/edited SVG (or PNG) on the map. |
| `assets/map.js` | The landscape itself: terrain, rivers, ocean, roads, regions, traffic, trees, labels. |
| `tools/build-exports-manifest.js` | Rebuilds `assets/exports-manifest.js` (run with Node). |
| `index.tiles.html` | The previous version with five separate tiles (kept for reference). `assets/roads.js` is only used there. |

## Coordinates

- The map canvas is **1600 × 1000** (`viewBox="0 0 1600 1000"`, `preserveAspectRatio="xMidYMid slice"`,
  so it fills the screen and crops the edges on other aspect ratios).
- The world origin is at canvas **(800, 500)**, the centre of the sign-in card.
- World axes: **x** runs right-down on screen, **y** runs left-down, **z** is up. `P(x, y, z)` converts world
  to screen. Roads, buildings and vehicles are aligned to x or y.
- `Assets.map.G(cx, cy)` converts a canvas pixel to the world `[x, y]` ground point. Regions are positioned
  by picking a spot on the canvas and converting it with `G`.

| Beira port, ocean, tanker, rig, small boats | bottom-left corner | `OCEAN` polygon, `BEIRA`, `quay()`. `tankerRun()` sails the tanker in, offloads at the jetty, sails out and returns (one 56 s loop). `boats()` adds six small boats on random sea lanes, new on every load |

| Pipeline Beira → Feruka → Msasa | bottom | `PIPE`, `pipeline()` |

The **ring road** is a rounded square at world `±RING` (370) around the card whose sides bend in and out. The bends are
the `BEND` knots per side: `[position along the side, offset]`, where a positive offset is away from the card. `side(name, c, lat)`
gives a point on it, `lane()` a driving lane, `RING_PTS` the whole loop. The pylons and the branch-road junctions follow
the bends. Branch roads are in `ROADS`. There are no dead ends: every road joins another, runs off the map, or ends at the gate of the place it serves. The Beira port road winds via `PORT_K`. Keep the bends gentle (an offset change
under ~0.3 per unit along the side), because vehicles keep their straight-axis sprites.

## Draw order

`render()` draws, in order:
1. `ground()` — grass, dry plain, land-use pads, ocean + beach, rivers, all roads (in world space, so the
   pattern textures are isometric).
2. `falls()` and `pipeline()`.
3. Everything that stands up (regions, mountains, pylons, traffic, trees), **sorted by depth `x + y`**
   (further back first). Each region is one item at its anchor.
4. Power-line wires and the text labels on top.

## Common changes

- **Move a region:** change its anchor, e.g. `const REF = G(1290, 268)`. Keep it clear of the ring road
  (world |x| or |y| ≈ 370) and update the matching tree keep-clear zone in `trees()` (`zones` / `rects`).
- **Add a building to a region:** add it inside that region's function using local coordinates
  (0,0 = the region anchor), e.g. `s += A.houses.rondavel(80, 20);`.
- **Traffic:** `drive(items, 'car' | 'truck', spec, pts, dur, begin)` drives a vehicle along a world polyline, such as a
  `lane(...)`. Inside `city()` and `suburb()`, use `go(kind, spec, localPts, dur, begin)`. A vehicle faces the main axis of its
  trip and fades in and out at each end. Each trip is cut into ~45-unit pieces, sorted by depth with everything else,
  so vehicles pass correctly behind or in front of buildings, mountains and trees. **Tankers are only drawn facing +x or
  +y, so give their routes in that direction** (a console warning flags a reversed one). **No collisions:** vehicles that share a lane
  must have the same `dur`, and vehicles that cross at a junction use lap times that repeat together (city: 12 s and 18 s)
  with `begin` offsets picked so they never meet. Retiming one can bring back crashes, so re-check crossings after a change.
  Pass `stops` (`{ pointIndex: seconds }`) to park a vehicle on its route, as the REF village truck does. Liveries: cars `zetdc`,
  `zetdc-truck`, `moepd`, `zera`; trucks `petrotrade`, `noic`.
- **Routes can turn corners:** each run with one heading gets its own sprite. An example is the ZESA truck's tour of the REF village.
- **Power lines:** `LINES` is a list of pylon chains, each starting at Hwange (or a pylon of the line it branches from) and
  running to a region or off the map edge. The wires' flow animation runs from the first pylon to the last. Trees keep clear of pylons.
- **City, suburb and REF village** are drawn as separate depth-sorted pieces (`put(localDepth, svg)`), not one block, so traffic can
  run between their buildings. Add new buildings there with `put` at their centre's `x + y`.
- **Trees:** `trees()` scatters up to 360 trees with a fixed seed, skipping roads, water, regions and the
  `zones`/`rects` lists. The dry plain gets baobabs/acacias, everywhere else pines and round trees.
- **Labels:** the `tag(cx, cy, text)` calls at the end of `render()` (canvas coordinates).

## Importing artwork from `exports/`

`exports/svg` and `exports/png` hold every asset as a separate file for editing in Illustrator, Figma,
Photoshop, etc. An edited file can replace the JS-drawn asset on the map.

### How the frame works

Each export was cut at the asset's bounding box plus 6 units of padding. Its `viewBox` was the asset's
own coordinate frame, with (0,0) at the ground anchor, at 4× pixel size. When Illustrator re-saves a file, it
rewrites the viewBox to `0 0 W H` and the frame is lost. `assets/exports-manifest.js` stores the original
frame for every file so it can be placed exactly where the JS version was drawn.

### To use an edited file

1. Save the edited file over the original in `exports/svg/<folder>/<name>.svg`. Keep the artboard the same size.
2. Run `node tools/build-exports-manifest.js` from the project root. If it re-saved a file that was not in the
   manifest before, add its frame to `FRAMES` in the tool. The frame is the original `viewBox` from before the
   edit, the same as an untouched sibling export of the same asset.
3. In `assets/map.js`, add the name to `USE_EXPORTS`, e.g. `'06-houses/school'`, and draw it with
   `swap(name, x, y, () => <original JS asset>)` at the same `x, y` the JS asset used. If the file is missing,
   `swap` falls back to the JS drawing.

Directly: `Assets.ex.place('06-houses/school', x, y, { format: 'svg' | 'png', scale, flip })`.

| `06-houses/school` | REF village, local (-96, 2) | "Lenah Primary School" version. The Illustrator re-save had left the waving-flag animation in the old 1× units, so the flag was drawn tiny in the corner. Its values were rescaled ×4 (original kept as `school.svg.bak`). |

Files that were also re-saved in Illustrator and have recovered frames in the manifest, but are **not** used

Moving vehicles still use the JS versions, because those spin their wheels and turn to face their direction of travel.

### Limits of imported files

- They are drawn with `<image>`. SMIL animation inside the file (`<animate>`) still plays, but the page's
  CSS classes (`spin`, `glow`, `flow`, …) do not reach inside, so CSS-driven motion in an edited file is frozen.
- If Illustrator scales artwork that has an `<animate>` on it, check the animation values were scaled too
  (see the school note above).
- `exports/png` files work too (`format: 'png'`) but are raster and heavy (≈1 MB each); prefer SVG.

## Running it

The page works from `file://` and from the dev server in `.claude/launch.json` (`node .claude/serve.js`,
port 5173). There is no build step, apart from the manifest tool above when export files change.

## Performance

The map has around 17,000 SVG elements and about 740 animations (each vehicle piece has its own motion). If it stutters on slower machines, the
cheapest savings are fewer trees (the `n < 360` limit in `trees()`) and fewer vehicles in `render()`/`city()`, or a larger `PIECE` length in `map.js`.

## Land textures

`assets/textures.js` paints each texture once when the page loads, saves it as a JPEG and registers it as a
world-space `<pattern>` (`tx-grass`, `tx-grass-dry`, `tx-lawn`, `tx-dry`, `tx-dirt`, `tx-sand`, `tx-gravel`,
`tx-field-soil`). They are plain images, so they cost nothing per animation frame. In total they add about
270 KB of inline data.

- **Tweak a texture:** edit its entry in `T`. `px` is the canvas size, `w` is how many world units one tile
  covers (bigger = coarser), `seed` sets the random layout, and `draw` sets the colour stops and the strokes
  (`blades`, `blobs`, `stones`, `cracks`).
- **Use one:** in `ground()` call `TX('dry', fallback)`. It returns `url(#tx-dry)`, or the fallback if the
  canvas was unavailable.
- **Soft zone edges:** `feather(d, fill, widths, opacity)` draws fading strokes of the same fill along an
  outline, so the zone blends into the grass. The dry plain also has a band of `tx-grass-dry` round it.

## Mountains

Mountains are screen-space shapes (`peak()` / `mountain()` in `map.js`), textured with `tx-mt-lit` /
`tx-mt-shade` (forest canopy) and `tx-rock-lit` / `tx-rock-shade` (granite). The rock is faded in near the
summit through `mask="url(#tx-topmask)"`.

They are **placed automatically** by `placeMountains(list, { box, avoid, rects })`. Each wanted
`[cx, cy, w, h, seed]` (canvas px) is moved to the nearest spot where the silhouette covers no road.
If nothing fits, the mountain shrinks. Peaks also keep apart from each other.
- `avoid`: canvas ellipses to keep clear (Beira/Feruka/Msasa depots, the suburb).
- `rects`: canvas boxes to keep clear (`CARD` = the sign-in card on a 16:10 screen).
- The pipeline may pass behind a mountain; roads may not. Roads come from `A.map.roadLines()`, so if a road
  moves, the mountains move off it on the next load.
- `A.map.mountains` holds the final positions (handy in the console). The MUTARE label follows the biggest peak.
